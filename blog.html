5 Simple Ways to Support a Loved One Aging at Home

Small changes make a big difference for an older adult who wants to stay at home safely.

  • Remove trip hazards. Loose rugs, cords and clutter are common causes of falls. Clear walkways and add night-lights in hallways and bathrooms.
  • Keep a routine. Regular times for meals, rest and activities bring comfort and make changes in health easier to notice.
  • Stay connected. Loneliness affects health. Short daily calls, visits or a companion caregiver help keep spirits up.
  • Organize medications. A weekly pill organizer and a simple written list help prevent missed or doubled doses. Ask the pharmacist to review the list.
  • Ask for help early. Family caregivers need rest too. Respite care for a few hours a week can prevent burnout.

This article is for general information only and is not medical advice. Talk with your healthcare provider about your specific situation.

Mind & Spirit

Caring for the Caregiver: Protecting Your Own Wellbeing

Looking after someone you love is meaningful work — and it can be exhausting. Here is how to keep your own cup full.

  • Notice the signs. Constant tiredness, irritability, poor sleep or withdrawing from friends can mean you need support.
  • Share the load. Make a list of tasks others can take on, and accept help when it is offered.
  • Protect small moments. A short walk, prayer, music or a quiet cup of tea each day matters more than you think.
  • Talk to someone. A counselor, pastor, mentor or support group can help you process what you are carrying.

This article is for general information only and is not medical advice. Talk with your healthcare provider about your specific situation.

Pain & Comfort

Living Well With Chronic Pain: Gentle, Non-Invasive Habits

Chronic pain touches every part of life. These everyday habits can complement the care plan from your healthcare provider.

  • Keep moving gently. With your provider’s approval, light activity such as walking or stretching can help reduce stiffness.
  • Practice relaxation. Slow breathing, guided relaxation and prayer or meditation can ease the tension that often comes with pain.
  • Rest well. A regular sleep schedule and a calm bedtime routine support the body’s ability to cope.
  • Track your patterns. A simple pain journal helps you and your provider see what helps and what makes things worse.

This article is for general information only and is not medical advice. Talk with your healthcare provider about your specific situation.
